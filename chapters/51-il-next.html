51. Where to go next

In this chapter the book hands you the keys. You have read every layer of IRON LEDGER; now you get a ladder of projects graded from an afternoon to a fortnight, each touching a layer you know, with the tests that will tell you when you are done. Then a reading order for the parts of the source this book did not cover, and a few words about what to read after that.

What you need from earlier chapters. All of Part 5, and the habit from Chapter 37 of letting the compiler and the test suite do the bookkeeping. Every project below is described in terms of the doors and phases you read in Chapter 42 through Chapter 47.

51.1 How to use the ladder

Each rung names a layer, a change, the files it touches, and the test that proves it. Do them in order the first time; each one assumes the muscle memory of the one before. Work in a copy of the game. When a rung says "the compiler lists…", read every error before fixing any; the list is the design checklist. When it says "write the test first", do: the shape of the tests in Chapter 49 (build a small state, run one thing, assert on state) is the shape you want.

Two rules from CLAUDE.md apply to every rung. No new randomness outside rng.zig's streams, and no floats in rules math. If you find yourself wanting either, you have found the wrong design, and the golden master will agree with the book.

51.2 The ladder

Rung 1 · A tuning knob (an hour)

Layer: data. Change: pick a constant in data/tables/tuning.zon (the founding funds, the decision window, a fatigue threshold), change it, and find the test that notices. Then make the same change in a mod directory and build with -Ddata. Files: one ZON file. Done when: you can name the test that pinned the old value and explain whether it should be updated or the change reverted. You did most of this in Chapter 40; the rung is worth repeating with a knob that has a formula behind it, so you follow the grep into the rule.

Rung 2 · A query line (an hour)

Layer: queries. Change: add something to a screen that the state already knows but no query shows. Ideas: a person's age in years on the People list (there is ageYears); the number of days until a contract's next battle on the Contracts screen; a company's total monthly payroll on the Forces detail pane. Files: queries.zig only. Done when: a test on a generated company asserts the exact text of the new line, and the client shows it (run --tui and look). This is Exercise 44.1 with a new subject.

Rung 3 · A command (an afternoon)

Layer: commands and the parser. Change: a new player action with a refusal. Ideas: set_leave_days (put a person on leave for N days, refusing if they are deployed), rename_person, pin_medbay_priority. Files: commands.zig (variant, arm, test), cli.zig (verb, parser branch, usage row, an errorText sentence for any new error). Done when: the compiler has walked you through the switch, the "every listed verb parses" test passes, your own test covers the refusal with expectError and the effect with expectEqual, and the verb works in both the REPL and the client's : line without either frontend being edited. Exercise 42.1 is the model.

Rung 4 · A tick phase (an afternoon)

Layer: the daily tick. Change: a small recurring effect. Ideas: a morale point each week for a company whose mess facility is at level 3 or higher and that is at home; a log line on the first of the month summarising hulls in the depot; a weekly decay of a new counter. Files: tick.zig (the phase and its place in advanceDay), possibly a field in state.zig. Done when: a test on a hand-built state proves the phase fires when it should and not otherwise, you have written one sentence saying why it sits where it does in the phase list, and the golden master still passes. Exercise 43.2 is the model; the harder part of this rung is choosing the phase order and defending it.

Rung 5 · A field that persists (a day)

Layer: persistence. Change: make the field from Rung 4 (or any new Person or Unit field) survive a save. Files: the domain struct, and in store.zig the DDL, a migration, the INSERT and the SELECT. Done when: a round-trip test sets the field, saves to ":memory:", loads, and reads it back; and the "v5 store upgrades in place" test still passes, proving your migration runs on an old file. Exercise 47.1 is the model. If the field is on Stats rather than a person, find how the stats are saved (as key-value rows in meta) and follow that pattern instead.

Rung 6 · An event (a day)

Layer: events. Change: a new entry in one of the decks in contract_events.zig, with options and effects, and if it needs one, a new Effect variant. Files: events.zig (the variant), contract_events.zig (the deck entry, applyEffectsFor), queries.zig (effectsText), and the 12.24 test's classification switch. Done when: the compiler has listed all three switches and you have answered each (how it is shown, what it does, whether it may be automatic), a test rolls the deck at your entry's number and asserts the effect on state, and the "automatic events never move money" test still holds. Exercise 46.1 showed you the checklist.

Rung 7 · A screen (two or three days)

Layer: the client. Change: a new pane on an existing tab, or a new modal. Ideas: a "recent battles" pane on the Contracts tab fed by queries.battleLog; a confirmation modal for trim_stock that shows what will be sent home before sending it. Files: app.zig (a drawX, a key arm, possibly a Modal variant), and a query if the data is not already exposed. Done when: the pane draws at every size tier without overflowing (run with the window at 80 by 24), the smoke script has a new assertion that visits it, and grep still finds no direct writes to g. in app.zig. Chapter 48 is the map; the discipline is to fetch everything through a query and act through exec.

Rung 8 · A subsystem with its own stream (a week)

Layer: all of them. Change: a mechanic that needs its own randomness. Ideas: a weather system that persists per planet and rolls daily (currently weather is rolled per battle); a rumour market that seeds contract offers with partial information; a morale event deck for companies at home. Files: a new Stream variant in rng.zig (the array grows on its own), a new file in src/sim with its own tests, a phase in the tick, one or two commands, a query, persistence for any state it keeps. Done when: the new stream is the only place your code rolls dice, every other stream's rolls are unchanged (write a test in the style of "streams are independent"), the golden master passes, the round trip passes, and a fresh reader can find your subsystem from root.zig and its //! comment. This is the rung at which you are no longer reading IRON LEDGER; you are extending it.

Rung 9 · A second frontend (a fortnight)

Layer: the boundary itself. Change: a new client that uses only the three doors. The natural one is a batch runner: read a script of verbs from a file, execute them through cli.parseCommand and commands.execute, print a query or two, exit with a status. It is the smallest possible frontend, and writing it proves that the boundary is complete: anything you find yourself needing from GameState directly is a missing query, and the fix is to add the query, not to reach past it. Done when: the runner can replay docs/repl_smoke.sh's script and produce the same landmarks, and you have added at least one query that the terminal client did not need but a batch runner does (a machine-readable summary, say).

51.3 What this book did not cover

Part 5 read the spine of the program. These parts of the source were mentioned but not read, and each is a self-contained afternoon:

  • src/gen/company_gen.zig and person_gen.zig. How a starter company is rolled: lances from the house's random assignment table, pilots with skills from an experience band, a support tail sized to the hulls. The heaviest user of the .generation stream, and the reason two campaigns with the same seed have the same people.
  • src/econ/logistics.zig and src/sim/network.zig. The star map's distances, jump counts, freight costs and the HQ link graph. Integer arithmetic on light-years and basis points, with a small graph search.
  • src/econ/market.zig and contract_market.zig. How boards refresh: rarity targets against 2d6, listings that age out, offers scaled by reputation and reach.
  • src/sim/maintenance.zig and hq_ops.zig. Weekly maintenance checks by tech skill against a target number, the repair and fabrication queues, construction projects with paperwork and build days.
  • src/domain/meklab.zig. The construction rules: engine and structure tables, criticals, the refit classes. A pure rules engine over a hull's slots, with tests against TechManual values.
  • src/tui/png.zig and emblem.zig. A PNG decoder with inflate written from the specification, and two terminal graphics protocols. Nothing to do with the game, everything to do with how far "no libraries" can go.

The reading order for the whole source, if you want to go from the top: ARCHITECTURE.md; src/root.zig; src/domain/types.zig; src/sim/rng.zig; src/sim/clock.zig; src/sim/state.zig (skim the fields, read the money and lookup helpers); src/sim/commands.zig (the union and a few arms); src/sim/tick.zig; one query in src/sim/queries.zig; src/persist/sqlite.zig; and finally src/tui/app.zig's run, draw and handleKey. That path is a couple of hours, and after it nothing in the repository will look unfamiliar.

51.4 After IRON LEDGER

You now know Zig as it is used in a program of real size: no hidden allocation, no hidden control flow, errors as values, data checked at compile time, and a test suite that turns design rules into build failures. Three places to take that next.

The standard library. You have used perhaps a tenth of it. It is written in the same style you have been reading and it is on your disk (zig env prints the path). Start with std/array_list.zig and std/hash_map.zig, whose APIs you know, then std/Io/Writer.zig, whose print you have called a thousand times. Reading how {s: <20} is parsed at compile time is a good afternoon.

The build system. Chapter 31 and IRON LEDGER's build.zig showed one module, one executable, one test step and one option. Real projects add dependencies from build.zig.zon, generate files at build time, cross-compile, and ship C libraries built from source. The build runner is ordinary Zig and its source is in the standard library too (std/Build.zig).

Your own program. The capstone in Chapter 38 was a mercenary ledger from scratch; the shape of IRON LEDGER is a way to grow it. A pure core with commands in and queries out, a seeded dice bag, an arena per campaign, integers for money, ZON for data, and a golden-master test from the first commit. None of that is specific to BattleTech. It is a way to make a program you can still reason about at thirty thousand lines, and you have now read the proof that it works.

Exercises

Exercise 51.1 · Plan a rung practice

Choose Rung 3, 4 or 6 and, before writing any code, write the plan as a list: the files you will touch, the switches you expect the compiler to list, the test you will write first, and the phase or arm your change belongs next to. Then do the rung and compare the list with what actually happened.

Solution

There is no single answer, but here is a plan for Rung 3 with set_leave_days that a reader of Part 5 should be able to write: (1) commands.zig: a variant set_leave: struct { person: types.PersonId, days: u32 }; the compiler will list the execute switch and nothing else. (2) The arm: validate the person exists (UnknownPerson), is active, and is not deployed (a new error PersonDeployed, or reuse CompanyDeployed); then set leave_until_day = gs.clock.day_index + days; then log with the company tag; return .{}. (3) cli.zig: verb leave exists already for the medbay modal, so check its parser branch before adding; the usage table and the verbs list. (4) A test: hire, set leave, assert the field; deploy a company and assert the refusal. (5) errorText for any new error. What actually happens: you discover that Person.isAvailable(day) already reads leave_until_day, so no phase needs changing, and that the client's People screen has an L key wired to a leave verb, which tells you the game may already have this command. Reading before planning is the lesson.

Exercise 51.2 · Find the missing query challenge

Begin Rung 9 with a thirty-line program: a new --script <file> flag in main.zig that reads verbs from a file, parses each with cli.parseCommand, executes it, and at the end prints the campaign's day and funds. The first time you reach for a GameState field to print, stop: is there a query for it? If not, add one. Which query did you add?

Solution

The loop is runRepl in main.zig with the prompt removed: tokenise each line with std.mem.tokenizeScalar, take the first token as the verb, call game.cli.parseCommand(verb, &it), and on a command call execute and print errorText on refusal. The day and funds are on queries.status already, so the honest answer for most readers is "none, once I looked". A batch runner that also wants a machine-readable summary (one line per company: name, hulls, people, local funds) finds that queries.summary returns prose, and adds a companiesBrief query returning a slice of small structs. That new query is then available to the terminal client for free, which is the whole argument of Chapter 44.

Check your understanding

  1. Which rung first requires touching more than one layer, and which layers?
    AnswerRung 3: the command layer (commands.zig) and the parser (cli.zig), plus an error sentence.
  2. What proves a migration works on an old save file, given that the round-trip test always starts from fresh DDL?
    AnswerThe "v5 store upgrades in place" test, which inserts hand-written rows in an old schema and opens the store, running every migration.
  3. Why does Rung 8 insist on a new RNG stream?
    AnswerDrawing from an existing stream would shift every later roll in that subsystem, changing saved campaigns' replays; a new stream leaves existing ones untouched.
  4. What does Rung 9 test that no Zig test does?
    AnswerThat the command/query boundary is complete: a second frontend that needs something not exposed reveals a missing query rather than a reason to reach into the state.
  5. Name the six design choices from IRON LEDGER that transfer to any program.
    AnswerA pure core with commands in and queries out; a seeded dice bag with named streams; an arena per campaign or session; integers for money; ZON data checked at compile time; a golden-master test from the start.
What you learned.
  • A nine-rung ladder from a data knob to a second frontend, each naming its layer, its files and the test that proves it.
  • The compiler's switch errors and the suite's pinned numbers are the checklist for every rung; the golden master and the round trip are the safety net.
  • The parts of the source this book skipped, each a self-contained read, and a reading order for the whole tree.
  • The standard library, the build system and your own program are the three places to take the habits you have learned.
  • The design of IRON LEDGER is not about BattleTech: it is a way to keep a large program readable, and you have read the whole proof.
